# The emotional palette — mixing humor, depth, life, death, dark and light, and when not to joke

A voice is not "friendly". It is a ratio, recorded in the Story Bible and delivered beat by beat.

## The axes

humor · depth · life · death · dark · light · hope · grief · warmth · competence · urgency

A Bible records the mix as percentages with named bands, for example "70 % warm-competent, 20 % dry humor,
10 % quiet gravity", plus **banned tones** ("never sarcastic at the user", "never cute in an error"). Two
projects may share axes; they must not share the ratio and the diction — that is what the voice registry's
uniqueness check compares.

## Turning a ratio into a draft

- The **dominant band** owns the opening line and the call to action.
- A **minority band** appears once per ~300 words, in one sentence, then yields. Ten percent gravity is one
  true sentence in a 400-word onboarding flow, not a paragraph.
- Bands do not blend inside a sentence. A joke and a grief note in one line cancel to nothing.
- The reader should be able to name the dominant feeling after one paragraph.

## Walter's hierarchy (Aarron Walter, *Designing for Emotion*)

Functional → reliable → usable → pleasurable. Copy earns the top layer only after the three below it are
true in that same piece of text: an error message must first say what happened and how to fix it; only
then may it have a personality. Pleasure on top of a failure reads as mockery.

## Humor that ages well, and humor that does not

| Ages well | Ages badly |
|---|---|
| Dry, specific, at the situation's expense | Puns, especially in errors |
| Self-aware about the product's limits | A mascot talking |
| One line, then back to work | Forced quirk in every string |
| Rooted in something the reader also noticed | A joke that needs the reader to know the team |

## When a joke is wrong — no exceptions

Crisis and therapy surfaces · legal, medical and financial consequence · an error that cost the reader
data, money or time · grief, illness, loss · security incidents · anything the reader is anxious about at
that moment (a failed payment, a locked account, a lost draft).

The test before any light line: **would this be fine if the reader is crying?** If not, cut it.

## Depth without heaviness

One true sentence, then move on. Depth is a well the reader glimpses, not a lecture. A mental-health
onboarding can say "some days you will not open this app, and that is fine" and then show the next screen.

## Light and dark

Dark is honesty about cost, difficulty, loss. Light is the after-state, the small win, the reader's own
capability. A piece that is all light is a brochure; all dark is a warning label. The Bible's ratio decides,
and the safety reference (`safety.md`) can override it toward light on vulnerable audiences.

## Recording it

`docs/story/story-bible.md` § Emotional palette holds the ratio, the banded description, the banned tones
and one example sentence per band. The humor/emotion reviewer audits a draft against that section, not
against taste.
