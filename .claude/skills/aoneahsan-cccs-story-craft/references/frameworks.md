# Story frameworks — seven structures and when each fits

A structure is a promise about the order in which the reader will feel things. Pick one per piece, state it
in the draft's self-check, and keep it. Mixing two structures reads as drift.

## The beat test that works on any of them

Pixar's writers and the *South Park* writers arrived at the same rule: consecutive beats must connect with
**"therefore"** or **"but"**, never **"and then"**. Read your draft's sections as beats. If two adjacent
sections join with "and then", one of them is a list item pretending to be a story; cut it or make it cause
the next one.

## Hero's Journey (Campbell, condensed by Vogler)

Twelve stages in fiction; five beats are enough for product content:

1. Ordinary world — the reader's life before the product, described in their own terms.
2. The call — the moment the old way stopped working (a specific failure, not "challenges").
3. The threshold — the first real use; what it cost to start.
4. The ordeal — the hardest part, honestly told.
5. Return with the elixir — what they can do now that they could not before, measured.

Fits: about pages, founder stories, case studies, a portfolio piece told from the client's side. The reader
is the hero; the product is never the hero (see StoryBrand in `copywriting-frameworks.md`).

## Dan Harmon's Story Circle

Eight steps on a clock: **You** (comfort) → **Need** → **Go** (unfamiliar situation) → **Search** (adapt) →
**Find** (get what they wanted) → **Take** (pay a price) → **Return** → **Change** (they are different). The
bottom half of the circle (Go/Search/Find/Take) is where the discomfort lives; content that skips it reads
as a brochure.

Fits: onboarding flows (each screen is a step), feature announcements with a "why we built it", long blog
posts. Onboarding maps cleanly: You = the welcome, Need = the problem screen, Go = first setup, Search = the
first attempt, Find = the first success state, Take = the honest limitation, Return = the home screen,
Change = the habit formed.

## Pixar Story Spine

"Once upon a time ___. Every day ___. One day ___. Because of that ___. Because of that ___. Until finally
___. And ever since then ___." Eight sentences; the two "because of that" beats force causation.

Use it as a **drafting scaffold**: write the eight sentences first, then expand. Use it as a **diagnostic**:
if a finished piece cannot be collapsed back into the spine, its middle is padding.

Fits: changelogs with an arc, launch posts, a case study's summary paragraph.

## Save the Cat (Snyder)

Fifteen beats; five carry over to content: **opening image** (the state of things in one picture),
**catalyst** (the event that makes change necessary), **midpoint** (a false victory or false defeat),
**dark night** (the honest low), **final image** (the same picture as the opening, changed). The opening and
final images should rhyme — the reader measures the change by the difference.

Fits: launch narratives, year-in-review posts, a rebuild story.

## Kishōtenketsu (起承転結)

Introduction (ki) → development (shō) → twist (ten) → reconciliation (ketsu). No conflict, no antagonist.
The "twist" is a shift of perspective that recontextualizes the first two parts; the ending reconciles them.

Fits: reflective blog posts, portfolio pieces about craft, and every **mental-health surface**, where
conflict framing ("fight your anxiety", "beat procrastination") is wrong and this structure gives the reader
a shape without an enemy. It also fits product philosophy pages.

## In medias res

Open in the middle of the action; backfill the setup only once the reader is already holding the moment.
The first sentence is a specific instant: a time, a screen, a sound, a number on a display.

Fits: hooks for anything — social posts, email openers, the first paragraph of a blog post. Rule: the
backfill must arrive within three sentences or the reader is lost rather than curious.

## Freytag's pyramid

Exposition → rising action → climax → falling action → dénouement. Old, symmetric, and exactly right when
there is a measurable outcome to land on.

Fits: case studies (the climax is the metric), incident write-ups, CV bullets in miniature — situation,
action, measured result (`surface-cv-resume.md`).

## Choosing

| You have | Use |
|---|---|
| A reader who changes because of the product | Story Circle or Hero's Journey |
| A launch, a release, a "what we shipped" | Save the Cat beats or the Pixar spine |
| A reflective or sensitive subject | Kishōtenketsu |
| One line to stop the scroll | In medias res |
| A number that proves the point | Freytag |
| A draft that feels like a list | The therefore/but test, then restructure |
