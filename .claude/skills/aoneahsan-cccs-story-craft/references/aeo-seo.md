# AEO and SEO for story content — be cited, not only ranked

The funnel (indexed → crawlable → renderable → ranking), the per-page content contract and the honest
position on what Google confirms live in `aoneahsan-cccs-react-seo`, `aoneahsan-cccs-seo-content-writing`
and `aoneahsan-cccs-react-aeo`. This reference is the story writer's slice: what changes in the *prose* when
a page must be cited by an answer engine. AEO is good SEO with a stricter opening, not a replacement for it.

## Why it matters, with the sources (all 2025–2026)

- **Cited brands earn the clicks.** Seer Interactive's 2026 update (53 brands, 5.47 M queries, 2.43 B
  impressions): brands cited in AI answers earned about 120 % more organic clicks per impression than
  uncited brands on the same query — per million informational impressions, roughly 20,700 clicks against
  9,400.
- **AI Overviews are on about half of queries.** BrightEdge's Generative Parser measured ~48 % of tracked
  queries in February 2026; Google has said roughly 50 % of US queries. Estimates by dataset range from
  15.7 % (Semrush, late 2025) to 60.3 % (Xponent21, US, April 2026) — measure your own share-of-answer
  rather than trusting one vendor's number.
- **Most citations are third-party.** AirOps (21,311 brand mentions): 85 % of AI brand mentions come from
  third-party pages. McKinsey's August 2025 AI Discovery Survey (1,927 consumers): a brand's own website is
  only 5–10 % of the sources AI search references. So content that other people quote matters more than
  content that describes itself.
- **AI referrals are small and convert.** Seer: AI-sourced sessions grew 527 % year over year (Jan–May
  2024 → Jan–May 2025). Ahrefs' first-party data: AI-referred visitors were 0.5 % of traffic and 12.1 % of
  signups.
- **Scannable, concise, objective copy is measurably more usable.** Morkes & Nielsen (Nielsen Norman
  Group): combining the three raised measured usability by 124 % — concise alone +58 %, scannable +47 %,
  objective +27 %.

Carry a number only with its source name beside it, as above. A number without a source is cut
(`aoneahsan-cccs-copywriting`).

## The levers, in the order they pay

1. **Answer-first.** Under a question heading, the direct answer comes before supporting detail. Then the
   nuance, then the story. An engine quotes the first sentence; a person reads the second paragraph.
2. **Question-based H2s**, written the way people type them. One question per section; the H2 set alone
   should read as a FAQ.
3. **Schema that mirrors visible content.** FAQPage, HowTo, Article, Product — only for content the page
   actually shows. Schema that says more than the page is a penalty, not a boost.
4. **E-E-A-T signals a machine can see.** A named author with a real bio, a dated update, cited statistics
   with links, an expert quote attributed to a person.
5. **Freshness.** A visible "updated" date that is true; a changelog line when the facts changed.
6. **Third-party mentions.** Content designed to be quoted: a definition, a benchmark, a named method.
   That is the 85 % lever, and no on-page trick replaces it.
7. **Entity consistency.** The same product name, spelling and one-line description everywhere
   (`aoneahsan-cccs-react-aeo`).

## What the story writer adds that the SEO contract does not

The contract guarantees uniqueness and structure; the story guarantees someone finishes the page. The
answer-first paragraph is where the two meet: it must be quotable *and* be the hook. Write it twice — once
as the flat answer, once with the specific detail that makes it yours — and ship the second.

## Never

Keyword stuffing (it lowers AI visibility) · a fabricated statistic · noindex to hide thin content · a
question heading whose answer is not in the next paragraph · schema for content the page does not render.
