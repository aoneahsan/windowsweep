# The tells

**Last Updated:** 2026-11-04

What gives generated work away, by surface. One tell is a reason to look again. Several together are a rewrite. This list
points at symptoms: removing them from a generic draft leaves a generic draft without symptoms, so go back to the project read.

## Words

The real examples come from a teammate's review of Shortlists: *"It reads as if claude has written it."*

| Tell | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| A phrase nobody in the trade uses | look it up | find their mobile number with Enrich |
| Vague about who does what | Roles that haven't changed it for themselves follow along. | Roles on the workspace rules get this change. Roles with their own rules keep them. |
| Explains the product to itself | Always on. The pipeline is the source of truth, no invite needed. | Always on. The role's status comes from the pipeline. |
| Abstract where it could be concrete | What this role does without being asked, per stage of {{pipeline}}. | What happens automatically on this role, stage by stage. |

Also:

- Hype and filler: seamless, effortless, powerful, robust, unlock, elevate, supercharge, delve, leverage, streamline. The full
  floor is `aoneahsan-cccs-story-craft` → `assets/banned-phrases.txt`.
- Rhythm: every sentence the same length; triads; mirrored pairs; "not X but Y"; "whether you're A or B"; em dashes as a habit.
- Shape: a summary line after a list; a closing line about what you can now focus on; a label plus a colon plus a slogan.
- Empty courtesy: "Please note that", "Oops!", "We're thrilled", "I hope this finds you well", exclamation marks for nothing.
- Vague verbs: helps, enables, allows you to, manage, handle. Say what happens.
- Stock UI words: Get started, Manage settings, Something went wrong, No data available. Name the thing and the next step.
- Translations done word for word. Each language gets the words its own users use.

## Screens

- Three (or six) equal cards of icon, title and one line.
- An icon in a tinted circle or square on every row.
- Sparkles, or a purple gradient, standing for anything AI.
- A pill badge on everything; every section in the same bordered rounded card; the same radius and shadow on all of it.
- Everything centred, or everything the same weight, so nothing is clearly the main thing.
- Decoration doing hierarchy's job: blobs, meshes, glass, loops that mean nothing.
- Hero, logos, features, testimonials, CTA, with nothing specific in any of them.
- An empty state of a grey icon and "No X yet", where it could say what's missing and offer the fix.
- Invented metrics on a dashboard. Every number is real, or it isn't there.
- Emoji as icons. Default library styling, untouched, on a product that's meant to have its own look.
- New styling on an existing product, when its own class strings were next door.

The method for a page's look: the anti-slop reference in `aoneahsan-cccs-design-process`.

## Flows

- A wizard for a one-step job, or a new path beside one the product already has.
- "Are you sure?" where Undo would do. A confirmation toast where the change on screen already says it.
- An onboarding carousel that teaches nothing, ending in "Get started".
- Asking for what the product already knows.
- Steps in the order a form builder would put them, where the work has its own order. Ask someone in the trade what happens first.
- Dead ends: a state with no way forward, or an error with no next step.
- A success page or confetti for routine work.

## Emails

- A subject line with a colon and a slogan. A greeting and a closing that could go to anyone.
- Newsletter styling on what should read as one person writing to another.
- A paragraph about how the product helps. Say what happened, and what they can do now.
- A signature from nobody, when a real person sent it.

## Images

- Robots, glowing brains, sparkles, circuit patterns, blue-purple gradient meshes, glossy 3D blobs.
- Generator artefacts: extra fingers, melted text, garbled UI, faces that are slightly off.
- Stock smiles, isometric people, a laptop showing a fake screen.
- Icons of mixed stroke, weight or style. A template shared with another product.
- A mocked screenshot. Use a fresh capture of the real app with believable data (`aoneahsan-cccs-store-listing-assets`).
- A raster doodle where the product's own marks and tokens would do. SVG master, always.

## Data anyone will see

- John Doe, Jane Smith, Acme, Foo, Lorem ipsum, test@test.com.
- Every name the same length; every number round; everything going up and to the right.
- Use believable people, roles and clients from the product's own world, at different lengths, with some of them awkward.

## Docs, PRs and tickets

- "Welcome to", "In this document", a wall of badges, emoji on every heading, "Features ✨".
- A closing summary of what the document just said.
- Marketing tone in technical writing. Say what it does, what it doesn't, and what to do next.
